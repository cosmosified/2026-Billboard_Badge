 1 Panel
 Common to both variants. Chapters 2 and 3 are self-contained and do not refer to one
 another.
 1.1 Geometry and bus
 960 × 672 pixels. Two COGs behind one FPC, each owning half of every scan line. A line is
 120 bytes: 60 to the master with CS1 low, chip select swapped, 60 to the slave with CS2 low.
 Register writes hold both chip selects low and so reach both COGs at once.
 Two buses share MOSI and SCK. Register writes and the OTP readback are bitbanged — a
 few dozen bytes, and the OTP needs a ~50 kHz clock with the data pin turned around for
 the read. The two 80 640-byte planes go out over hardware SPI at 4 MHz. The GPIO matrix is
 re-pointed between the two; chip select stays plain GPIO, because it is swapped every 60
 bytes.

 Register writes hold both chip selects low, so one write reaches both COGs. That is also how a single 0xB0 write loads
 the same waveform table into both halves of the panel.
 1.2 Pixel encoding
 A pixel is a pair of bits, one in each plane, and the pair selects a waveform table as
 (red << 1) | bw. Red is therefore not black with red on top: a red pixel has the red bit
 set and the bw bit clear.

 Three frames pushing at the rail, one resting at ground. The rest is the point: a long run at VDH drags the
 slower red pigment along behind the black, and the rests let it relax back.
 Four bytes become four phases and a repeat count. Frame counts are nibble-packed, high nibble
 first, so a single phase is capped at 15 frames.

 The two tables are mirror images through the shake, then separate: paper to VDL with a rest at

 2.5.5 Red variants
 Red on this film is not a third direction. Both coloured pigments carry the same charge and
 both rise on a positive field; they differ only in threshold, red's being lower. On VDH black
 comes up with the red and has to be pulled back out, and the pull that removes black
 removes red with it — the two differ in speed by about 2.5, not the ten to one the literature
 suggests. So red is driven on VDHR, where black never comes up at all.
 Groups 0 and 1 are never rewritten: putting the red stages over group 1 would replace nine
 repeats with one, which means grey blacks and red landing on an undriven pixel. The
 rewrite starts at group 2.

 A whole byte per count, so 45 frames fits here where the 084 is capped at 15 by its nibble packing. ph2 is

 Every level this driver writes has the form AB AB, so phases 0 and 2 share a level and phases 1 and 3
 share one. That matches the two identical triples: each is a drive followed by a rest.

 These are not the same axis. On this COG the table length does not set the refresh length, so only a prefix of the

 Identical to the 084: 0x09←7F, 20 ms; 0x05←7D; 0x09←00, 200 ms; DC and both chip

 5 Refresh control
 5.1 Mode and red handling
 A fast refresh is black and white. The short waveform cannot make a good red: on the 084
 the red table is barely reached in the time the short table runs, and on the B1 the fixed
 window leaves red pale and getting worse with every frame that half-drives it. Red content
 is drawn as ink instead of being dropped, so the picture stays readable and simply goes
 monochrome until the next slow refresh.
 Red already on the film is the harder case. A few clearing groups are not enough —
 measured on the panel the red survives them, and once it has sat through one fast frame it
 does not come out of the next one either. So the first fast refresh after a red frame silently
 runs on the panel's own waveform. The promotion is to the waveform only; red stays
 suppressed, which is also what stops it repeating.

 5.2 Session and latching
 The panel latches one waveform per session and will not take a second one. Rewriting
 0xB0 inside an open session does not re-latch it — it drops the panel back onto its own
 waveform. Anything that needs a different table therefore closes the session first: a change
 of mode, or on an 084 a change in whether the table carries red.
 With the session held open, a later image costs only its plane data and a bare 0x15: no
 supply cycle, no reset, no register sequence, no soft start. The session is torn down every
 20 images regardless.

 7 Errata
 1. Supply polarity does not fail like a power fault. With the enable inverted the OTP read still
 succeeds, because it runs during a window where the pin happens to sit at the other level.
 The failure appears only at the soft start, as a booster that never reports ready.
 2. 0xB0 has one window. Before the last load strobe it is overwritten by the OTP waveform;
 after the soft start it is too late; inside an open session it does not re-latch.
 3. A zero group is not an empty group. It runs about four seconds.
 4. Group timing applies to every table. A table left at one frame of ground ends the group
 there whatever the others asked for, VCOM included.
 5. EPD_T_PRE_SS is in units of 10 ms, unlike every other timing constant in the configuration
 header. The value 50 produces 500 ms, which is what both sequence tables show.
 6. Both plane bits set lands on table 3, which no waveform drives.
 7. "Red is on the panel" has to outlive RAM. The film is not reset by the MCU losing its
 memory, and a wake that believes the panel is red-free will run a fast refresh straight over
 red that nothing short can remove. The flag is kept in RTC memory.
 8. Do not release the bus pins while the panel is off. Sound in theory — driving an
 unpowered COG backfeeds it through the ESD diodes — and it broke this panel: the refresh
 reached the soft start and BUSY never came ready. Only the two shared bus pins change
 hands between bitbang and SPI.
 9. On a B1, do not skip 0x70. Without it the panel draws about 2 A during refresh.
